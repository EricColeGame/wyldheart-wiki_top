If you've gone looking for Wyldheart classes, you've probably noticed something strange: this game doesn't really have them. Wyldheart, the co-op fantasy RPG from Wayfinder Studios, throws out fixed jobs and hands you a fully classless skill system instead. That single design decision reshapes everything about how you build a hero, which is why so many players searching for Wyldheart classes end up asking the same question — "So what am I actually supposed to play?"

The short answer is that you play whatever combination of skills, weapons, and gear you want. The longer answer involves five skill trees, a set of weapon classes, three ancestries, and 19 backgrounds that give your character a story before you ever set foot in a dungeon. This guide walks through how the system works, which roles emerge in practice, and how to plan a party that functions in 1–4 player co-op.

## Why Wyldheart Classes Are Classless (And Why That Matters)

Most fantasy RPGs ask you to lock in an identity within the first five minutes, often before you understand how combat even feels. Wyldheart does the opposite. You begin as a humble peasant tossed into a dungeon, tasked with recovering one of the ancient Relics of Caerwyn to protect your town. Nobody hands you a title, a guild badge, or a predetermined role.

Instead, you choose an ancestry, pick one of 19 backgrounds for roleplay flavor, and then build outward through 50+ RPG skills spread across five trees. The result is a game where "what class are you?" is a question with dozens of valid answers — and most of them are hybrids.

| Common player question | How Wyldheart answers it |
|---|---|
| "What class should I pick?" | There is no class pick — you choose an ancestry and one of 19 backgrounds |
| "Am I locked into one role?" | No: 50+ RPG skills sit in five flexible, classless trees |
| "Can I mix healing, magic, and melee?" | Nothing stops you from splitting points across Weaponry and Magic |
| "How do I try something new?" | Weapons swap quickly in combat, and the trees are built for experimentation |
| "Do I have to reroll to change my build?" | Campaigns are deliberately short, so testing new ideas is cheap |

That last point matters more than it sounds. Because a campaign runs roughly 10 hours of story with about 15 hours of optional content, you can finish a run, learn what you liked, and start fresh without feeling like you wasted a hundred hours.

## The Five Skill Trees That Shape Every Wyldheart Build

Rather than classes, Wyldheart gives you five skill trees: **Adventuring**, **Survival**, **Dungeoneering**, **Weaponry**, and **Magic**. The official [Wyldheart site from Wayfinder Studios](https://wyldheartgame.com/) describes them as "fully flexible," meaning you can specialize deeply in one or create clever combinations across several.

| Skill tree | What it broadly covers | Builds it tends to support |
|---|---|---|
| Adventuring | Exploring the world, uncovering quests, and getting around towns and wilds | Scouts, quest-chasers, utility characters |
| Survival | Gathering materials, crafting, cooking, and enduring harsh regions | Crafters, support players, long-haul explorers |
| Dungeoneering | The ruins and dungeons themselves, plus the hazards waiting inside | Trap-aware explorers and relic hunters |
| Weaponry | Melee and ranged combat techniques | Frontline bruisers and skirmishers |
| Magic | Spellcasting and supernatural effects | Pure casters and hybrid spellblades |

A practical rule of thumb: spreading points evenly across all five trees usually produces a character who is mediocre at everything. Most satisfying builds pick two trees as their backbone and dip into a third for utility. If you want to throw concoctions while also swinging a sword, Survival and Weaponry make a natural pairing; if you want to nuke from range and still survive a dungeon, Magic plus Dungeoneering covers both damage and navigation.

Because the trees are classless, the "best" combination depends entirely on your group. A solo player benefits from self-sufficiency, while someone in a four-player party can afford to go all-in on one narrow specialty and let teammates cover the gaps.

## Weapon Classes: The Closest Thing to Traditional Wyldheart Classes

If you need something concrete to anchor your build, look at weapon classes. Wyldheart advertises **5+ unique weapon classes to master**, and combat is designed around swapping between them fluidly. During a single fight you can switch from a melee weapon to a bow, cast a spell, and lob a deadly concoction — all without visiting a menu screen.

| Combat style | How it plays | Pairs well with |
|---|---|---|
| Melee weapons | Close the distance and trade blows up front | Weaponry + Survival for staying power |
| Ranged weapons | Keep enemies at arm's length and chip them down | Weaponry + Adventuring for mobility |
| Spellcasting | Burst damage and magical utility from a safe distance | Magic + anything you enjoy |
| Thrown concoctions | Alchemical damage and disruption mid-fight | Survival + Magic for a hybrid alchemist |
| Constant swapping | Change tools as the fight shifts instead of committing | Any two trees you like |

This is why "Wyldheart classes" is a slightly misleading search term. What the game actually gives you is a combat toolkit. Your "class" is whatever you happen to be holding when the dungeon door opens — and because swapping is fast, that can change mid-encounter.

## Ancestries and Backgrounds: Where Your Build Really Begins

Before skill points ever enter the picture, Wyldheart asks who your character is. You select one of three ancestries — **Freefolk**, **Grimhorn**, or **Mossling** — and then choose from 19 backgrounds that shape your roleplay hooks and how your story starts.

| Choice | Options | What it contributes |
|---|---|---|
| Ancestry | Freefolk, Grimhorn, Mossling | Your character's identity — a proud Freefolk, a towering Grimhorn, or a little Mossling |
| Background | 19 humble backgrounds | Roleplay flavor and the "who were you before this" question |
| Skills | 50+ RPG skills across five trees | The mechanical backbone of your build |
| Weapon classes | 5+ unique classes | Your moment-to-moment combat feel |

The official material frames these as flavor-first choices rather than rigid stat packages. In practice, that means you should pick the ancestry and background you find most fun to roleplay, then let the skill trees do the heavy lifting. If you're the type who agonizes over optimization, resist the urge to treat this as a class selection screen — it isn't one.

Worth noting: Wyldheart is set in a mythic fantasy world steeped in Fey folklore, and it's being published in partnership with Curve Games. The demo lets you explore the Whisperwood region and take on challenging dungeons, which is the fastest way to feel how the ancestries and trees interact before release.

## Party Builds and Co-op Tips for Wyldheart Classes

Here's where the classless design really shines. Wyldheart supports solo play and up to four-player online co-op with seamless drop-in/drop-out, cloud-shared saves, group XP, and scaling difficulty. That means nobody gets left behind if they miss game night — the party can keep going, and the absent player comes back leveled.

### Five community-style roles that cover a co-op party

These aren't official classes; think of them as role labels you can aim for with skill points.

| Role label | Primary tree | Weapon focus | Why it helps the group |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frontline bruiser | Weaponry | Melee | Holds the attention of enemies so squishier builds can work |
| Ranged skirmisher | Weaponry | Bows and thrown weapons | Punishes enemies from angles the bruiser can't cover |
| Spellcaster | Magic | Spells | Bursts down priority targets and adds utility |
| Alchemist support | Survival | Concoctions | Crafting, cooking, and disruptive throws keep the party supplied |
| Dungeon scout | Dungeoneering | Whatever's lightest | Finds ruins, quests, and routes the rest of the party would miss |

### Practical co-op advice

- **Let difficulty scale for you.** Wyldheart scales encounter difficulty with party size, so a duo or trio isn't secretly punished for not filling all four slots.
- **Assign crafting duties.** Cooking meals for your party and upgrading gear are party-wide benefits, not solo chores — someone should own it.
- **Embrace the short campaign.** With about 10 hours of story and 15 hours of optional content, a campaign is a great excuse to test two or three different builds rather than perfecting one forever.
- **Use cloud saves as insurance.** If one player can't make it, the group can continue without them and nobody loses progression.
- **Don't duplicate everything.** Since Wyldheart classes don't exist in the traditional sense, the fastest way to a weak party is four identical generalists. Split your trees.

## FAQ

**Do Wyldheart classes exist at all?**
Not as traditional, locked-in job classes. Wyldheart uses a classless system built on five flexible skill trees — Adventuring, Survival, Dungeoneering, Weaponry, and Magic — plus 5+ weapon classes you can swap between freely. Your effective "class" is the combination you assemble.

**How many weapon classes are in Wyldheart?**
The game advertises 5+ unique weapon classes to master, covering melee weapons, ranged weapons, spellcasting, and thrown concoctions. Since you can swap between them mid-fight, most players end up proficient in more than one.

**Which ancestry or background should I pick?**
There's no confirmed "best" option. Freefolk, Grimhorn, and Mossling are presented primarily as character identity choices, and the 19 backgrounds are roleplay hooks. Pick the one whose story you want to tell, then build your skills around the combat style you enjoy.

**Can I change my build later, or am I stuck?**
The skill trees are described as fully flexible, and the campaigns are intentionally bite-sized at roughly 10 hours of story plus 15 hours of optional content. If a build isn't clicking, finishing the run and starting a new character is a realistic option rather than a huge time investment.

**Is Wyldheart good for solo players?**
Yes — the game is designed for solo play as well as up to four-player online co-op, with scaling difficulty. Solo players will likely want a broader spread of skills, while co-op groups can afford to specialize hard.

Wyldheart is currently listed for PC on Epic and Steam, with a release date still to be announced, so expect the details around Wyldheart classes and skill trees to keep evolving as the demo and showcases roll out. For now, the smartest approach is simple: pick the ancestry and background that sound fun, choose two skill trees to build around, and stay flexible with your weapons. In a game that refuses to hand you a class, your build is whatever you make of it.

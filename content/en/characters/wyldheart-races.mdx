Picking your character's ancestry is the very first decision WYLDHEART asks you to make, and it quietly shapes everything that follows. The Wyldheart races — officially labeled ancestries — come in three flavors: the proud Freefolk, the little Mossling, and the towering Grimhorn. Get that choice right and your first campaign clicks; get it wrong and you may spend ten hours wondering why your hero never quite felt like yours.

That's the reason this guide exists. Below you'll find everything currently known about the Wyldheart races, how ancestries interact with the game's 19 backgrounds and 50+ skills, which pick suits which playstyle, and how to build a co-op party that works when four people with four different schedules finally sit down to play.

## What Are the Wyldheart Races? Ancestries Explained

Wayfinder Studios doesn't use the word "race" in its official materials — the game calls its three playable peoples **ancestries**. The distinction is small but genuinely useful when you're planning a build. In WYLDHEART, your ancestry describes *who your character is*, not *what your character can do*. There are no ancestry-locked classes, no exclusive weapons, and no skill tree hidden behind a specific heritage.

That design choice matters. The skill system is classless and split across five branches — Adventuring, Survival, Dungeoneering, Weaponry, and Magic — so a Mossling can absolutely specialize in heavy melee weapons, and a Grimhorn can lean into concoctions and spellcasting. Your ancestry sets the tone of your story; your skills decide how that story is told in combat.

Here's the confirmed lineup at a glance:

| Ancestry | Official Descriptor | Relative Scale | Overall Impression |
|---|---|---|---|
| Freefolk | "A proud Freefolk" | Human-scale | Grounded, defiant, classic fantasy hero |
| Mossling | "A little Mossling" | Small | Fey-touched, curious, underdog energy |
| Grimhorn | "A towering Grimhorn" | Large | Imposing, physical, front-line presence |

The descriptors in that table come straight from official WYLDHEART copy. The "overall impression" column is interpretation — the studio hasn't published a stat breakdown, so treat any numbers you see elsewhere with suspicion.

## Freefolk, Grimhorn, and Mossling: A Closer Look

### Freefolk

The Freefolk are the closest thing WYLDHEART has to a familiar fantasy baseline. Official material describes them as proud, and they fit the game's opening premise perfectly: you start as a humble peasant thrown into a dungeon and tasked with recovering one of the ancient Relics of Caerwyn to protect your town. A Freefolk hero crawling out of that dungeon reads as a classic underdog arc.

If you're the type of player who wants a character that feels grounded in the world rather than exotic to it, this is your starting point.

### Grimhorn

*Towering* is the operative word. The Grimhorn are the physically imposing option, which makes them the natural pick for anyone who wants to be the wall between the party and whatever is in the next room. WYLDHEART's combat is fast and action-oriented, with quick swapping between melee weapons, ranged weapons, spells, and thrown concoctions — so a big, aggressive front-liner fits the pacing nicely.

One open question: whether a larger silhouette affects hitboxes or collision in combat. That's the kind of detail the limited demo should answer, and it's worth testing before you commit to a full campaign.

### Mossling

The little Mossling is the most intriguing of the three, largely because of the setting. WYLDHEART takes place in a mythic fantasy world steeped in Fey folklore, and the Mossling name — small, natural, faintly mischievous — sits right in that tradition. Players who want to lean into the stranger, more folkloric side of the Kingdom of Caerwyn will probably gravitate here first.

Small stature doesn't mean small ambitions. With classless skill trees, a Mossling can be the party's deadliest damage dealer, its best cook, or its most relentless dungeon crawler.

## How Wyldheart Races Fit Into Character Creation

Character creation in WYLDHEART is built from a handful of layered choices. Ancestry is only the first one, and it's arguably the least mechanically binding:

| Creation Pillar | Options Available | What It Actually Controls |
|---|---|---|
| Ancestry | 3 (Freefolk, Grimhorn, Mossling) | Identity, tone, and roleplay flavor |
| Background | 19 | Where your story begins and how you're framed |
| Skills | 50+ across five trees | How you fight, survive, and explore |
| Weapon classes | 5+ | Your moment-to-moment combat feel |
| Areas to discover | 100+ | The scale of your campaign |

The takeaway: your ancestry choice is not a power decision. It's a personality decision. Because the skill trees are fully flexible and classless, you can specialize hard in one branch or mix Adventuring, Survival, Dungeoneering, Weaponry, and Magic into a hybrid that no one else at the table is running.

If you've played tabletop RPGs, think of ancestry as the character concept you pitch in session zero, and skills as the sheet you actually build over the next ten hours.

### Tips Before You Roll

- **Play the limited demo first.** It covers the Whisperwood region and challenging dungeons, which is the fastest way to feel how each ancestry moves and fights.
- **Don't optimize for a tier list.** None exists yet, and the classless system makes ancestry a weak lever for power anyway.
- **Pick the background that sounds interesting, not the one that sounds strong.** All 19 are narrative starting points.
- **Test weapon swapping early.** Melee, ranged, spells, and concoctions are all available, and the swap speed is central to the combat feel.
- **Engage with towns.** Villagers have their own schedules, and quests unlock through eavesdropping, deciphering rumors, and poking around strange ruins.
- **Craft and cook.** Meals for your party and upgraded gear both matter before you push deeper into danger.

## Building a Co-op Party With Different Wyldheart Races

WYLDHEART is designed for busy adults, and the co-op structure reflects that: up to four players, seamless drop-in/drop-out, cloud-shared saves, group XP, and difficulty that scales with party size. That means nobody gets left behind if a friend misses a session — the party can keep going, and the absent player rejoins without a penalty.

Because ancestries carry no mechanical restrictions, party composition is about playstyle coverage rather than racial bonuses:

| Party Size | Suggested Approach | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 player | Any ancestry | Difficulty scales down for solo play |
| 2 players | One front-liner, one flexible build | Grimhorn plus Mossling is a natural split |
| 3 players | One of each ancestry | Maximum variety in tone and roleplay |
| 4 players | Duplicates are fine | No stacking or exclusivity rules have been announced |

A few practical co-op tips from the design itself: group XP means everyone levels together regardless of who landed the killing blow, and cloud-shared saves mean the host isn't a single point of failure. Plan around roughly 10 hours of story if you want to finish, or closer to 25 if your group chases every optional area.

## FAQ: Wyldheart Races Answered

**How many Wyldheart races are there?**

Three. Wayfinder Studios calls them ancestries: the Freefolk, the Grimhorn, and the Mossling. Alongside them, you choose from 19 backgrounds that determine how your story begins.

**Are the Wyldheart races locked to specific classes?**

No. WYLDHEART uses a classless skill system with 50+ skills spread across Adventuring, Survival, Dungeoneering, Weaponry, and Magic. Your ancestry doesn't gate any of them, so any character can build toward melee, ranged, magic, or concoctions.

**Which Wyldheart race is best for a first playthrough?**

The Freefolk are the most approachable if you want a traditional hero arc, but there's no wrong pick. The limited demo in the Whisperwood region is the best way to sample the feel before committing to a full campaign.

**Can I change my ancestry after starting a campaign?**

That hasn't been confirmed. Given how flexible the skill system is, ancestry appears to be a flavor and identity choice rather than a permanent mechanical commitment — but until the full release, plan on it being locked in at character creation.

The short version: the Wyldheart races aren't a difficulty setting, they're a storytelling choice. Pick the one whose story you want to tell, then build the skills to match — and if you're playing with friends, remember that drop-in co-op means there's always room for one more hero in Caerwyn.
